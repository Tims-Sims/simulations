## Universal Game-Like SIMS Rules

### One focused game, not a unit examination

- Assess only the objective or small objective cluster named in each unit’s Selected SIMS Focus.
- Use one continuous mission and one primary mechanic. Do not divide the build into disconnected rounds.
- Use no more than four gameplay screens, plus one mission screen and one completion screen. Target 6–8 minutes.
- Avoid long passages, objective-definition matching, dense checklists and long internal scrolling.

### Game feel

- Every accurate move visibly changes the game state: light, movement, unlocking, assembly, reveal or stabilisation.
- The learning interaction must drive the game; do not place ordinary questions over decoration.
- Do not use mandatory timers, lives, speed pressure or permanent failure. Provide retries and reduced motion.
- Use meaningful mission progress rather than unrelated points.

### Inputs and marking

- Use controlled taps, drags, reordering, fixed choices, punctuation slots, highlights or supplied tiles only.
- No unrestricted typing, subjective grading or human marking inside the SIMS.
- Every action has one exact answer or exact accepted set. Track first attempt separately from final mastery.
- Incorrect feedback states what is wrong, why, how to fix it and one corrected model.

### Silent, visual and accessible

- Do not use narration, read-aloud, voices, music, sound clues, microphone, recording or speech recognition.
- Represent speaking/listening concepts with captions, visual profiles, posture, expression, pace bars or syllable guides.
- Include Exit at top right, Rules, Back, Hint, Try Again and text-labelled progress.
- Use British spelling, modern Form 2 visuals, inclusive Caribbean contexts, 44 × 44 px targets, keyboard support and non-colour status cues.

# Unit 13 — A Process Approach to Writing Stories - Drafting, Revising and Reflecting on Stories

### Rewritten SIMS

- Story Pit Stop
- Previous concept retired: Story Patch Notes

## 1. Unit Objectives

- 13.1 Verbs - Progressive and Perfect Tenses: What are the progressive and perfect tenses of verbs, understanding the structure of these tenses, exploring the uses of these tenses in fiction texts, identifying these tenses in texts, using these tenses in sentences.
- 13.2 Writing a Strong Opening: Using visual texts to introduce setting and character through descriptive words, sensory details, dialogue, adjectives,adverbs, literary devices, diverse vocabulary and sentence structures.
- 13.3 Developing Dialogue and Action: Using dialogue and action to move the plot forward from rising action, climax, falling action, resolution. Form 1 All Objectives Draft 2
- 13.4 Using Figurative Language: Adding imagery and figurative language (similes, metaphors, personification) at every appropriate/relevant stage of the story.
- 13.5 Revising and Publishing Your Story: Reviewing the plot, character development, sequence as well other technical features such as organization, transitions, grammar and mechanics, using a checklist.
- 13.6 Reflective Writing: My Story Choices: Writing reflections on strengths, challenges and creative decisions in writing stories, understanding the value of reflective writing as a means for personal progress and empowerment.

## 2. Selected SIMS Focus

- Objectives assessed: 13.2, 13.3, 13.4, 13.5
- Focus: Strengthening a story opening, using dialogue and action to move plot, adding effective figurative language and revising with a checklist.
- Other unit objectives remain taught and assessed in the lesson, but are not added to this focused SIMS.

## 3. SIMS Identity and Student Goal

- SIMS name: Story Pit Stop
- Term: Term 1
- Student goal: Use the selected skill accurately to complete the game mission.
- Real-life/game context: A short story race has stalled. At each pit stop, the student swaps one weak sentence for the exact stronger sentence so the story car can continue.

## 4. Core Game Mechanic and Win Condition

Primary mechanic: Tap the replacement part for the displayed story line. A correct repair moves the car to the next pit stop.

### Win condition
Complete four exact repairs and reveal the revised story paragraph.

Black-and-white marking: Every tap, drag, order, label or replacement is checked against the exact answer stated below. No partial or subjective pass state.

## 5. Quick Rules

- A strong opening introduces setting, character or tension quickly.
- Dialogue and action should change the situation or reveal character.
- Figurative language must fit the scene; revision improves clarity, sequence and mechanics.

## 6. Screen-by-Screen Flow and Exact Build Content

### Screen 1 — Mission Brief

Story draft title: “The Last Bicycle”. Show a car at four pit stops: Opening, Action, Imagery, Final Check.

### Screen 2 — Opening Repair

Weak line: “It was a day and Jalen had a bicycle.” Replacement choices: “Rain hammered the galvanised roof as Jalen stared at the bicycle chained outside the closed shop.” [correct]; “Jalen was there.”; “Bicycles have wheels.” Correct repair introduces setting, character and tension.

### Screen 3 — Dialogue and Action Repair

Weak line: “‘We should do something,’ Maya said.” Choices: “‘The drain is rising,’ Maya shouted, grabbing the bolt cutters. ‘Help me free the bicycle before the water reaches the chain.’” [correct]; “‘Okay,’ Maya said.”; “Maya explained everything for three paragraphs.” The story car moves because the line reveals urgency and action.

### Screen 4 — Imagery Repair

Sentence frame: “The floodwater ___ around the tyres.” Options “coiled like a dark rope” [correct simile]; “was water”; “sang a happy birthday song” [reject as unsuitable]. Punctuation check: quotation marks and comma before dialogue tag must be exact in “‘Move back,’ Jalen warned.”

### Screen 5 — Final Checklist Repair

Display revised paragraph with one error: “Jalen had freed the bicycle, but Maya are still pulling the chain.” Correct replacement: “Jalen had freed the bicycle, but Maya was still pulling the chain.” Checklist items to approve: sequence is clear; dialogue moves action; figurative language fits; tense and punctuation are accurate. All four required.

### Screen 6 — Completion

Show the story car crossing the finish line and the revised paragraph. Skills: choosing stronger narrative lines; revising grammar, sequence and mechanics.

## 7. Unit-Specific Corrective Feedback

### Weak replacement selected

- Why: The line remains vague or does not develop setting, character or tension.
- How to fix: Choose the sentence with specific sensory and action details.
- Correct model: “Rain hammered the galvanised roof...”

### Dialogue does not move plot

- Why: The line does not change the situation or show a decision.
- How to fix: Select dialogue tied to an immediate action.
- Correct model: “Help me free the bicycle...”

### Revision error missed

- Why: The verb form does not agree with the subject or the story tense.
- How to fix: Replace “Maya are” with “Maya was”.
- Correct model: “Maya was still pulling the chain.”
